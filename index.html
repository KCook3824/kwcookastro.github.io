Background

About Me

I am a Ph.D Candidate in the Department of Physics and Astronomy at the University of Louisville in Louisville, Kentucky. My Ph.D work is focused on the neutral gas content of galaxies and its connection to the star formation rate. Spectral Energy Distribution fitting drives a large portion of my work, with the aim of accurate recovery of star formation histories. I am also interested in the chemical evolution of galaxies and the subsequenct enrichment of the circumgalatic and intergalactica medium. Additionally, the development of both the poeple and the instrumentation of the future of astrophysics is of high interest.

I received my B.S. in Physics from Western Kentucky University under the mentorship of Dr. Michael Carini, where I studied Blazars, the extreme end of the AGN types where a jet is directed at the Earth.

I expect to complete my PhD in Spring 2027 and am actively exploring postdoctoral opportunities.

Science

Research Interests

I tend to find myself drawn to population-scale questions — how galaxies evolve, how gas reservoirs correlate with star formation rates across cosmic time, mapping the chemical evolution of the IGM/CGM, and how we build and improve the instrumentation that enables us to answer those questions.

🌌
Galaxy Evolution

Understanding the structural and morphological evolution of galaxies across cosmic time using large photometric surveys. SED fitting for the estimation of galaxy parameters and the recovery of star formation histories. Disentangling quenching vs rejuvenating populations in the green valley.

⭐
Star Formation & Gas

Member of the LADUMA team studying the neutral hydrogen content of galaxies using the MeerKAT array. Examining how the neutral gas content of a galaxy influences and controls its star formation rate.

🔭
Astronomical Instrumentation

Interested in astronomical instrumentation including upcoming NASA missions including the Habitable Worlds Observatory. Previously worked on the MooSci lunar scintillometer for the site characterization of the Giant Magellan Telescope location.
