Background

About Me

I am a PhD Candidate in the Department of Physics and Astronomy at the University of Louisville in Louisville, Kentucky. My Ph.D work focuses on the relationship between the neutral gas content of a galaxy and its star formation. I'm also interested in the chemical enrichment and evolution of galaxies and their surrounding space.

I received my BS in Physics from Western Kentucky University under the mentorship of Dr. Michael Carini, where I studied Blazars, the extreme end of AGN where a jet is aimed directly at the Earth.

Beyond research, I'm passionate about science communication, mentoring the next generation of astronomers, and building community within academic spaces.

I expect to complete my Ph.D in Spring 2027 and am actively exploring postdoctoral opportunities.

Science

Research Interests

I tend to find myself drawn to large-scale questions — how galaxies evolve as a population, how gas reservoirs correlate with star formation rates across cosmic time, and how we build and improve the instrumentation that lets us ask those questions at all.

🌌
Galaxy Evolution

Understanding the structural and morphological evolution of galaxies across cosmic time using large photometric and spectroscopic surveys. Including work with self-organizing maps applied to the GAMA survey.

⭐
Star Formation & Gas

Examining how cold gas reservoirs regulate star formation rates on galactic scales, including resolved and integrated studies of the interstellar medium and its relationship to stellar populations.

🔭
Astronomical Instrumentation

Design and characterization of astronomical instruments, including the MooSci lunar scintillometer and MASS-DIMM turbulence profiling at the GMT site in Chile.
